# India airborne C5ISR gap: why there is no full-scale sovereign SCALE node

**Compiled.** 2026-07-22  
**Purpose.** Research note for the tri-service SCALE UAS investor case. Indian geography, Indian procurement, Indian think-tank and service reporting. Not a US CONOPS transplant.  
**Status labels.** confirmed | reported | synthesis (mission logic)

---

## 0. What the claim must mean (and what it must not)

**Unsafe claim:** “India has no ISR” or “India has no C5ISR.” False. The services already fly Israeli Heron / Heron Mk II and Searcher fleets, induct Drishti-10 (Hermes 900 licensed production), operate crewed Netra AEW&C and P-8I maritime patrol, buy emergency SATCOM-linked Heron Mk II after Operation Sindoor, lease/operate MQ-9B precursors, and field ground and air-defence fusion (IACCS–Akashteer–Trigun). Satellites and a growing HAPS/airship push sit above the UAV layer.

**Accurate claim for the SCALE case:**

> India still has **no indigenous, full-stack, long-endurance airborne C5ISR platform in operational service** — a sovereign SCALE-class node that owns airframe IP, mission computer, autonomy, multi-sensor payloads (EO/IR + radar + SIGINT/ELINT as fitted), SATCOM, and open interfaces into Army / IAF / Navy C2, at endurance and altitude that meet Joint Services Qualitative Requirements for persistent border and maritime watch.

That is the gap. It is not “no cameras in the sky.” It is **no Indian-owned flying data-centre and radio mast** that the services can scale, upgrade, and export on their own industrial terms.

**Working definition of “full-scale C5ISR platform” (India-relevant).**

| Layer | What “full-scale” requires | What India largely has today |
| ----- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Persistence | ~24–40 h orbits over LAC/LoC sectors or EEZ stations | Import MALE with SATCOM (Heron Mk II); indigenous MALE not inducted to SQR; indigenous HALE absent from inventory |
| Sensors | Concurrent EO/IR + SAR/GMTI + SIGINT/ELINT power and apertures sized in | Mixed; best stacks on foreign airframes; licensed builds without deep aerodynamics/avionics ToT (IDSA) |
| Mission stack | Indian-owned mission computer, fusion, autonomy, payload ICDs | Weakest link for indigenous programmes; shell-maker risk on many bids |
| Networking | Clean handoff into IACCS / service C2 / fires networks | AD fusion improved (Sindoor); below-brigade and sensor-to-shooter still incomplete (ORF) |
| Controller role | Direct attritables / launched effects from standoff | Demonstrated in concept during Sindoor-era ops; not yet a massed indigenous mothership fleet |
| Sovereignty | Upgrade cycle, source code, 60%+ IC path, export licence control | MQ-9B contracted but foreign; deliveries 2029–30; TAPAS mission-mode closed |

## 1. Why the gap exists — structural reasons (India)

### 1.1 Propulsion and airframe physics beat programme hope

TAPAS-BH-201 (Rustom lineage) showed ATOL and partial ISR integration, then failed Joint Services Qualitative Requirements on **altitude and endurance**. ADE leadership publicly named the **engine** as the primary constraint. Prototypes reached roughly **18 hours at ~28,000 ft** against a goal nearer **24 hours at ~30,000 ft**; all-up weight grew (reported ~2,850 kg). Mission-mode closure followed in **January 2024**. Without a credible turboprop map for Himalayan density altitude and maritime loiter, “full-scale C5ISR” never gets past the airframe gate. (ORF Bommakanti; DRDO/press reporting; IDSA Desai 2026.)

### 1.2 DPSU / single-lab development cycle without competitive pressure

For two decades the only serious indigenous MALE attempt sat inside DRDO/ADE. ORF notes a ~13-year path from launch toward 2016 first flight, cost revision to ~₹1,786 crore, and results the services judged inadequate. CENJOWS (2026) states bluntly: **no indigenous MALE-class UAV inducted after two decades**. That is institutional, not only technical: one developer, rigid SQRs, weak spiral path until private dual-award competition opened.

### 1.3 Certification and airworthiness compress slowly

Large unmanned aircraft need CEMILAC design approval, DGAQA production quality, software assurance, and hundreds to thousands of flight-test hours. Emergency buys of foreign types (Heron Mk II, Drishti-10) move faster under EP rules and existing STANAG/OEM pedigrees. Indigenous first-of-type cannot skip that queue. Qualification scarcity — not demand scarcity — is why ten bidders still collapse to a shortlist.

### 1.4 Licensed production ≠ platform ownership

Adani–Elbit Drishti-10 / Hermes 900 fills an operational hole. IDSA Desai is explicit: licensed production **does not transfer cutting-edge aerodynamics and avionics**. The sensor OEM and foreign software house still own the upgrade annuity. That is assembly, not a full-scale sovereign C5ISR business.

### 1.5 Component sovereignty and China-risk in the civil drone supply chain

MoD warnings against PRC-origin parts for military UAVs collide with an industry that, per private founders cited by ORF, still saw large China shares in commercial drone BoMs. De-risking raises cost and schedule. Full-scale military C5ISR stacks (radios, cameras, mission computers, crypto) cannot sit on that BoM without accepting compromise.

### 1.6 Import stopgaps mask the industrial gap — and arrive late

The **31 MQ-9B** tri-service contract (15 Navy / 8 Army / 8 IAF) is the clearest admission that the services needed SATCOM-enabled, high-endurance multi-role ISR **now**. Deliveries begin **2029**. ORF frames near-continuous LAC / Tibetan Plateau watch as a **key weakness** the SkyGuardian tranche is meant to redress. Import fills capability; it does not create an Indian platform-owner. Atmanirbhar tension is structural, not rhetorical.

### 1.7 Network architecture lags weapons and cameras

Carnegie India’s Sindoor lessons put **persistent ISR** and **networking** above any single airframe. ORF’s “Weapons Without Networks” line is India-specific: IACCS–Akashteer–Trigun performed; the **below-brigade tactical digital layer** and long-delayed Tactical Communication System still leave sensor-to-shooter incomplete. Army LVCCS work after Sindoor exists precisely because drone/radar tracks do not yet flow cleanly into automated fires. A “full-scale C5ISR platform” without hooks into that Indian C2 fabric is just another video feed.

### 1.8 Layer confusion: AEW&C, satellites, HAPS, tactical drones

India is correctly buying **layers**, not one silver bullet:

- Netra AEW&C — theatre air picture (crewed; not a SCALE substitute).
- Military satellites / SBS Phase-III plans — revisit and strategic ISR; orbital limits and ASAT risk.
- AS-HAPS / stratospheric airship Make-I (~₹15,000 Cr reported) — months-long persistence, still developmental; USI notes institutional/integration gaps for maritime use.
- Tactical UAS and loitering munitions — local, attritable; lose wide-area heavy-sensor persistence.

The missing middle for day-to-day **retaskable, multi-sensor, networked endurance** over borders and EEZ remains the SCALE / MALE–HALE airborne node under Indian control.

## 2. What the Indian military is missing out on

### 2.1 Geography makes persistence non-optional

- Land border **15,106.7 km** (MHA), including China **3,488 km** and Pakistan **3,323 km**.
- Coastline **11,098.81 km**; EEZ ~**2.3 million km²**.

Crewed sorties and short-endurance tactical drones cannot hold continuous orbits across that map. Gaps in time are gaps in warning.

### 2.2 Army — LAC / LoC watch and fires cueing

**Missing without a full-scale node:**

- Day-long standoff orbits that keep tracks on PLA/PA movement without crossing the border.
- All-weather SAR/GMTI when cloud and valley geometry defeat EO (Sindoor-era mountain ISR stress).
- Reliable SATCOM BLOS when ridgelines kill LOS radios.
- Clean tracks into artillery / long-range fires networks (LVCCS ambition) for TA, BDA and correction.
- A mothership that tasks attritables while the expensive airframe stays in permissive airspace.

**What they use instead:** Heron / Heron Mk II (foreign upgrade cycle), emergency Drishti-10, forthcoming MQ-9B SkyGuardian (from 2029), tactical UAS for local sectors. Coverage exists; **sovereign scale, commonality, and deep stack ownership** do not.

### 2.3 Air Force — standoff ISR, relay, strike support

**Missing:**

- Persistent ELINT/ISR orbits that fighters and scarce AEW&C cannot economically hold for days.
- Communications / tactical-data relay for dispersed packages.
- Sensor support to find–fix–track–assess without claiming Netra’s air-battle role.
- Homogeneous networking of a heterogeneous fleet (Carnegie: integration tempo is itself a readiness metric).

ORF: near-continuous LAC / plateau surveillance has been a **key IAF/Army weakness** the MQ-9B buy is meant to address — proof of the gap in service language, not vendor language.

### 2.4 Navy — maritime domain awareness continuity

**Missing:**

- Continuous offshore orbits over Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal approaches cheaper than P-8I hours.
- Surface search + relay + (where doctrine and payloads allow) ASW cueing to MH-60R / P-8I.
- Density of stations that match EEZ size; leased MQ-9B experience already showed value before the 15-airframe order.

P-8I remains superior for some high-end crew judgment missions. It does not erase the need for unmanned persistence.

### 2.5 Joint — common operational picture and controller economics

**Missing:**

- A **controller / C5ISR node** role that Sindoor highlighted: large platforms cue effects; expendables absorb attrition; guns and layered AD kill swarm mass.
- One industrial type that Army, IAF and Navy can sustain without three logistics tribes (IADB note on fragmented Heron / Hermes / Searcher fleets).
- An upgrade annuity that stays inside Indian software and mission-system ownership when threat libraries change.

## 3. How a SCALE-class platform fills the persistence and situational-awareness gap

SCALE (Specialised C5ISR Autonomous Long Endurance) is the product name for the missing middle: **not** a bomb truck, **not** AEW&C, **not** a tactical quadcopter, **not** a satellite.

### 3.1 Persistence surveillance

| Need | Without SCALE-class sovereign node | With SCALE-class node |
| ---- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| LAC sector hold | Sparse orbits; weather gaps; foreign SATCOM fleet limits | 24–40 h orbits, SAR + EO/IR, retaskable |
| LoC / western desert | Emergency Heron top-ups | Continuous watch + SIGINT as fitted |
| EEZ surface picture | P-8I pulses + limited MALE | Persistent track maintenance at lower $/hour |
| Crisis surge | EP imports, 12-month delivery caps | Domestic rate production after qualification |

Geography is the requirement document. Persistence is the answer that cheap drones and LEO revisit cannot fully replace when commanders need **immediate retasking** and **heavy multi-sensor loads**.

### 3.2 Situational awareness (Indian C2 reality)

SCALE fills SA only if it is designed as a **network citizen**:

1. Publish tracks into **IACCS** (air picture) and service ISR cells.  
2. Feed Army **Battlefield Surveillance / CIDSS / future LVCCS** for fires.  
3. Feed Navy coastal and fleet C2 / IFC-IOR-relevant MDA streams.  
4. Carry **relay** payloads where terrain or EMCON breaks LOS.  
5. Host **fusion and autonomy** that reduce crew count per orbit — India’s manpower and training constraint on large UAV fleets.

Without those interfaces, more endurance only creates more unfused video. Carnegie and ORF both say networking is the binding constraint; the airframe is the carrier of the network edge.

### 3.3 Controller / mothership role (post-Sindoor)

Sindoor showed massed cheap drones dying to upgraded legacy guns and layered AD, while precision and ISR mattered more than any single platform brand. SCALE’s investment thesis is to **own the standoff brain**:

- Find and hold.
- Cue shooters and attritables.
- Assess damage.
- Stay alive by not penetrating dense IADS.

That role is as relevant over Punjab and Ladakh as over the Arabian Sea — which is why the indigenous MALE AoN is **tri-service**, not Navy-only.

### 3.4 Sovereignty and industrial fill

MQ-9B closes a **capability** gap from 2029 with 31 aircraft. The **87-aircraft IDDM** programme (reported >₹30,000 Cr, 60% IC, dual award) is meant to close the **industrial and scale** gap. A full-scale C5ISR SCALE entrant fills both only if it is a **platform-owner**: mission computer, autonomy, payload interfaces, and software support in Indian hands. Otherwise India repeats the Heron pattern — operationally useful, strategically rented.

### 3.5 Honest limits (keep the claim clean)

SCALE does **not** replace Netra, P-8I high-end ASW judgment, fighter reconnaissance over defended airspace, or future HAPS months-long stare. It sits in the gap those systems leave every day: **days of persistence, heavy payloads, retaskable orbits, deep Indian network integration, sovereign upgrade path.**

## 4. One-page thesis for the business case (paste-ready)

**India’s forces already buy ISR. What they still lack in service is an indigenous full-stack long-endurance C5ISR airborne node — a SCALE-class flying mission system — at the altitude and endurance the Joint Services require.**  

Reasons are Indian and structural: turboprop and high-altitude performance shortfalls (TAPAS), long single-track DRDO cycles, CEMILAC-class qualification time, licensed builds without deep ToT, China-exposed component chains, and a C2 fabric that still struggles to turn tracks into fires below brigade.  

Geography (15,000+ km land border, 11,000+ km coast, 2.3 m km² EEZ) and recent combat (Sindoor’s demand for persistent ISR and networking) make the gap operational, not theoretical.  

Imports (Heron Mk II, Drishti-10, MQ-9B from 2029) and layers (Netra, P-8I, satellites, HAPS) patch pieces. They do not create a sovereign platform-owner for the decade of aircraft revenue and the multi-decade mission-systems annuity.  

That is the hole the tri-service indigenous MALE/SCALE competition exists to fill — if the winner owns the stack, not only the airframe.
