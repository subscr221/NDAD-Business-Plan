# SCALE UAS investor business case (tri-service)

**Class.** Specialised C5ISR Autonomous Long Endurance (SCALE) unmanned aircraft systems.**Anchor market.** India across Army, Air Force and Navy; selected exports after a domestic reference.**Audience.** Defence-literate lead investors and generalist co-investors.**Numbers.** Sourced in the appendix. Verified July 2026; re-check at use. The scenario in §7 is an illustration from disclosed assumptions, not a forecast.

> **C5ISR** is Command, Control, Computers, Communications, Cyber, Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance: the sensors, radios, computers and decision aids a force uses to see and direct. A **SCALE UAS** is a large unmanned aircraft in the roughly one-to-five-tonne bracket that stays aloft for about 24 to 40 hours and does that work. It carries radar and electro-optics, collects signals, relays communications, runs mission computers, and increasingly directs smaller drones. Treat it as a flying data centre and radio mast, not an aircraft with sensors bolted on.

## Executive summary

India already fields ISR. Israeli Herons, licensed Drishti-10s, crewed Netra and P-8I, and a foreign MQ-9B fleet arriving from 2029 all put cameras and radars in the sky. What India still does not have in operational service is an **indigenous full-stack long-endurance airborne C5ISR node**: a sovereign SCALE-class platform that owns the airframe IP, mission computer, autonomy stack, multi-sensor payloads as fitted, SATCOM, and the interfaces into Army, Air Force and Navy command networks. That hole — not a shortage of cameras — is what the live indigenous programme exists to fill. Heron, Drishti and MQ-9B prove demand and patch coverage. They leave the upgrade annuity with foreign OEMs.

The customer is already paying into the class twice, and both buys are tri-service. On 15 October 2024 New Delhi signed roughly ₹28,000 crore (about USD 3.5 billion) for 31 MQ-9Bs: 15 SeaGuardians for the Navy and eight SkyGuardians each for the Army and the Air Force, plus a separate performance-based logistics contract. Deliveries begin in 2029. On 5 August 2025 the Defence Acquisition Council accorded Acceptance of Necessity for Medium Altitude Long Endurance remotely piloted aircraft for all three services. Defence reporting puts the live programme at 87 aircraft, valued above ₹30,000 crore, under the Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured category with a 60 percent indigenous-content mandate, dual award planned at 64 to 36, and bids closed in mid-June 2026 with roughly ten Indian firms competing. Geography matches the money: 15,106.7 km of land border, 11,098.81 km of coastline, and an exclusive economic zone of about 2.3 million km².

**Governing thesis.** In this class the scarce asset is certification and qualification capacity, not demand. The entrant that converts sovereign tri-service demand into a certified platform first takes a decade of high-price, low-volume aircraft revenue, then a multi-decade annuity in mission systems, software and sustainment. The airframe is how an entrant gets in. The C5ISR stack is where the business sits.

Three claims support that thesis:

1. **Demand.** Funded and tri-service. Sixteen of thirty-one imported MQ-9Bs go to Army and Air Force. The 2025 AoN names all three services. Emergency Heron Mk II and Drishti-10 buys already put the endurance class over land borders and coastal water.
2. **Qualification.** Five to seven years of military airworthiness, with TAPAS as the cautionary timeline, turn a crowded bidder list into a shortlist. Financing the wait is the first diligence question.
3. **Returns.** Sustainment, software and upgrades run at roughly 8 to 12 percent of fleet value per year across a ~25-year life. The platform-owner of the mission computer, autonomy stack and payload interfaces collects that stream. The assembler of an airframe around licensed foreign sensors does not.

May 2025 (Operation Sindoor) sharpened the product one notch further. Large platforms directed effects from standoff while expendables struck and swarms died to upgraded legacy guns. Carnegie India's post-conflict review stresses persistent ISR and networking more than any single airframe. The durable role is controller and C5ISR node — over Punjab and Ladakh as much as over the Arabian Sea.

This is a sector case. It names no company. Diligence consolidates in §9.

## 1. The sovereign hole

The unsafe claim is “India has no C5ISR.” False. The accurate claim is narrower and sharper:

> India still has no indigenous, full-stack, long-endurance airborne C5ISR platform in operational service — a sovereign SCALE-class node that owns airframe IP, mission computer, autonomy, multi-sensor payloads as fitted, SATCOM, and open interfaces into Army, Air Force and Navy command networks, at endurance and altitude that meet Joint Services Qualitative Requirements for persistent border and maritime watch.

IDSA notes no indigenous HALE in inventory and full HALE dependence on foreign OEMs pending MQ-9B. CENJOWS (2026) notes no indigenous MALE-class UAV inducted after roughly two decades. ORF frames near-continuous LAC and Tibetan Plateau surveillance as a key weakness the SkyGuardian tranche is meant to redress — service need language, not brochure language.

**Why the hole exists**

- **Propulsion and performance.** TAPAS-BH-201 demonstrated automatic take-off and landing and partial ISR integration, then failed altitude and endurance JSQRs. ADE named the engine as the primary constraint. Prototypes reached roughly eighteen hours near 28,000 feet against a goal nearer twenty-four hours at about 30,000 feet. Mission-mode closure followed in January 2024.
- **Single-track development.** For years the serious indigenous MALE attempt sat inside DRDO/ADE. Competitive private dual-award is the institutional correction — not proof the product already exists.
- **Qualification time.** CEMILAC design approval, DGAQA production quality and software assurance do not compress because a tender is fashionable. Emergency foreign buys move faster on existing pedigrees.
- **Licensed production is not platform ownership.** Drishti-10 fills an operational hole. IDSA is explicit that licensed production does not transfer cutting-edge aerodynamics and avionics. The upgrade annuity stays abroad.
- **Networks lag sensors.** Carnegie India's Sindoor lessons put persistent ISR and networking above any single airframe. Tracks from drones and radars still do not flow cleanly into automated land fires. More endurance without Indian C2 handoffs is more unfused video.


| Need                  | Without a sovereign SCALE node                                                    | With one                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Border persistence    | Sparse orbits; weather gaps; foreign upgrade cycles                               | 24–40 hour multi-sensor orbits; Indian software path         |
| EEZ surface picture   | P-8I pulses plus limited import MALE                                              | Persistent track maintenance at lower cost per endurance hour |
| Situational awareness | Sensors that do not publish cleanly into IACCS, Army fires nets or Navy MDA cells | Network citizen into service C2                               |
| Controller economics  | Large foreign ISR plus separate attritable fleets                                 | Standoff brain that owns find–cue–assess                    |
| Industrial fill       | MQ-9B from 2029 (31 aircraft); Heron/Drishti patches                              | 87-aircraft IDDM path — only if the winner owns the stack    |

SCALE does not replace Netra, high-end P-8I judgment, fighter reconnaissance over defended airspace, or future HAPS months-long stare. It sits in the gap those layers leave every day. MQ-9B closes a capability gap from 2029. The indigenous programme is meant to close the industrial and scale gap. A SCALE entrant fills both only as a platform-owner. Otherwise India repeats the Heron pattern: operationally useful, strategically rented.


## 2. Three services, one class

The hole hurts three customers who already buy the same endurance physics.

**Army.** 15,106.7 km of land border, including 3,488 km with China and 3,323 km with Pakistan. After eastern Ladakh (2020) and Sindoor, Heron Mk II and Drishti-10 emergency buys, plus eight MQ-9B SkyGuardians on the 2024 import. Need: day-long standoff orbits on the LAC and LoC, all-weather SAR/GMTI, SATCOM beyond ridgelines, clean tracks into fires networks, a mothership for attritables. Configuration risk: high-altitude performance and army C2 can erode commonality if they become a second airframe.

**Air Force.** Already flies Herons; takes eight MQ-9B SkyGuardians; ORF frames the buy as closing LAC/plateau ISR gaps from Indian airspace. Project Cheetah's arming plan was reported shelved — SATCOM and sensors continue; armed work shifts to MQ-9B and loitering munitions. Need: persistent standoff ISR/ELINT, relay, find–fix–track–assess support. Hard limit: not Netra, not fighter reconnaissance, not a routine IADS penetrator. Soft risk: will reject a maritime-optimised airframe that cannot talk to air C2 and EW libraries.

**Navy.** Clearest published need. Fifteen of thirty-one MQ-9Bs; early Drishti-10 at Porbandar for Arabian Sea coverage. Need: MDA and surface search across ~2.3 million km² of EEZ, persistent track maintenance, relay, ASW cueing where payloads and doctrine support it. Natural first *export reference* (maritime surveillance travels better politically than armed land-strike). Domestically, no longer the only buyer that matters.

The commercial argument for one platform is simple. Separate service airframes triple NRE, triple flight-test campaigns and fragment sustainment. A common airframe, propulsion, flight controls, power system, mission computer, autonomy trunk, ground-control architecture and open payload interfaces can carry three packages — if those packages are configurations, not separate programmes. Where that claim fails (propulsion envelope, salt vs ice, crypto/C2 stacks the services will not share), treat it as a diligence risk, not a brochure feature.

**Diligence focus.** Documented Army / Air Force / Navy capture plan — or an honest single-service focus with a matching cost base. No navy-only story that undersells the Indian order book.

## 3. What SCALE is (and is not)

A decade ago investors filed large military drones under one word: MALE. The label now bundles two businesses with opposite economics.

The first is the armed drone over a contested battlefield. Houthi forces claim more than twenty MQ-9 Reapers shot down over Yemen since October 2023. US officials have acknowledged roughly a dozen by early 2025, then further losses in the spring 2025 campaign, at about USD 30 million per aircraft. A slow aircraft the size of a small airliner does not survive over a dense air-defence network. Loitering munitions have further eroded MQ-9-class strike as a growth thesis. Pitches for penetrating strike in this weight class deserve softening.

The second business is persistent C5ISR — the work historically associated with Global Hawk-class systems, mostly hidden from public view. SCALE names that business so it can be underwritten on its own terms.

**Specialised.** Designed around land and maritime surveillance, signals intelligence, communications relay and battle-management support — not a bomb truck with a camera. Power budgets sized for radar and mission computers. Antenna apertures designed in. Open payload bays. Satellite datalinks as core.

**C5ISR.** Revenue sits in radar, signals payloads, mission computers, datalinks and fusion software. Whoever owns that stack owns the margin. India's tender language has flagged control over design, software and data systems as a selection differentiator.

**Autonomous.** Flies itself for most of the sortie; small crew supervising several aircraft. Persistence and crew economics — not lethal independence. A human stays on the loop for use of force.

**Long Endurance.** Roughly 24 to 40 hours. Fuel fraction and wing design do not miniaturise. That physical barrier is why cheap tactical drones do not erase the class on land or at sea.

Sindoor sharpened the product without changing the thesis. Large platforms found and fixed; expendables struck; swarms died to guns. Attrition does not delete the large aircraft. It moves the aircraft from hunter to controller. Controller is the better business: higher software content, deeper C2 integration, stickier revenue. Open payload bays and software-defined mission systems are requirements of the class, not options.

**Diligence focus.** Mission set weighted to persistence, standoff SIGINT, relay and drone-controller roles. Written limits: no AEW&C replacement claim; no routine IADS penetration; no “India has no ISR” overclaim.

## 5. Qualification gate

> **Military airworthiness in plain terms.** Before a force accepts a large unmanned aircraft over its territory, a national authority must certify the design, the software and the production process — design reviews, ground rigs, hundreds to thousands of flight-test hours. In India: CEMILAC (design) and DGAQA (quality). STANAG 4671 helped Drishti-10 move under emergency rules. Certification is a gate passed more than once, and again when mission packages change.

Qualification organises this sector. It costs years and capital, and it has already consumed the state champion. TAPAS-BH-201 ran for more than a decade, missed altitude and endurance targets, closed as a mission-mode project in January 2024, and by March 2026 was reported excluded from the indigenous MALE competition. It survives as a technology path into Archer-NG. Design, prototype, flight test and qualification do not compress because a tender is fashionable. Before anything else, the business under evaluation is a business of financing a wait.

That wait creates the prize. Few entrants can fund it, so each sovereign market ends with two or three qualified suppliers. India has formalised that with dual award. Schedule slip destroys more value than routine cost overrun. At catalogue prices of ₹150–210 crore, a twelve-month slip defers hundreds of crores of annual deliveries at the ramp in §7.

For SCALE, autonomy and mission software must be certified with the airframe (DO-178C-class work). That raises the toll. It also deepens the moat for anyone who builds a certification-grade software organisation early.

**Diligence focus.** Milestones achieved versus claimed, with dates. Flight-test hours on representative prototypes — including high-altitude evidence if Army is claimed. Named CEMILAC/DGAQA engagement. Software certification bench sized for the job.

## 6. Platform-owner vs shell-maker

Two companies show up wearing the same class label. One collects the annuity. The other mostly does not.

The **platform-owner** holds airframe IP, flight-control software, mission computer, payload interfaces and autonomy. New radar modes, army fire-support adapters and air-force datalink loads are billed work on owned software. Mid-life upgrades sell into the installed base.

The **shell-maker** assembles an airframe around licensed foreign sensors. Assembly margin is real; emergency orders happen; then the sensor OEM sells the upgrades. Recompetes collapse to price. Drishti-10 is the living exhibit: operationally useful, strategically rented.

India's MQ-9B purchase came with a day-one logistics contract on the order of ₹4,350 crore — roughly 15 percent of acquisition value before any aircraft arrived. Across defence aerospace, sustainment, spares, training, software support and upgrades typically run at about 8 to 12 percent of installed fleet value per year over ~25-year lives. On the illustrative fourteen-aircraft fleet in §7, that annuity reaches roughly ₹200 to 300 crore a year by Year 10 before mid-life upgrades. Fourteen airframes look small on a slide. Modelled as a locked-in mission-systems annuity, they are a business. Only the platform-owner collects most of it.

**Commonality as a diligence risk (not a brochure feature).** An honest commercial pitch is often one airframe family with two to three qualified configuration baselines — heavy reuse on everything that does not touch salt, altitude or crypto — not a single baseline with free bolt-ons. The gates that most often force a second trunk: propulsion / performance envelope; maritime corrosion vs high-altitude cold and ice; C2/crypto/EW stacks the services will not share; operating concept (expeditionary field vs airfield vs coastal).


| Fork pressure             | Army                             | Air Force                              | Navy                                                          |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Performance / environment | Density altitude, ice, cold-soak | Shared land basing if map matches Army | Salt, corrosion, coastal/deck handling                        |
| Mission computer / C2     | Fire-support adapters            | IACCS, waveforms, IFF, EW libraries    | Fleet/coastal C2; shipboard EMI                               |
| Payloads                  | SAR/GMTI, land SIGINT, EO/IR     | Standoff ISR/ELINT                     | Maritime surface search; ASW kit only if doctrine supports it |

**Commonality test.** If more than a modest share of NRE is rewriting the flight-control or mission-computer baseline for each service, the company is building three aircraft. If differences sit in payload manifests, software loads and interface adapters, the one-platform thesis still holds. Ask for part-number reuse %, reused qual evidence, and marginal NRE before treating commonality as an investment fact.

**Diligence focus.** Platform-owner in substance — not licensed assembly alone. Import share of BoM against the 60% IC mandate. Software headcount as a real fraction of engineering. Commonality NRE disclosed, not assumed away.

The shape matters more than the totals. Three years of zero product revenue while orders accumulate. First delivery turns on the annuity. From mid-decade, support grows faster than aircraft revenue in percentage terms. Closing backlog sits near several years of forward revenue.

> **Pre-build test.** Roughly 3× first-year delivery capacity on order before first ship. Here: six units ordered by end of Year 3 against one delivery in Year 4.

> **BoM-margin trap.** Fully loaded gross margin of 35 to 50 percent at maturity is the credible band. BoM-only margins near 70 percent omit labour, integration, flight-test acceptance, warranty and amortised development.

**Sensitivities.** (1) Qualification slip (+12 months): first delivery to Year 5; Year-10 fleet to 11; Year-10 revenue to ₹684 crore in the checker — slip dominates cost-overrun risk. (2) Dual-award price compression: stress ASP to ₹150 crore. (3) Commonality erosion: extra ₹200–400 crore NRE for service-specific baselines can erase early equity value. (4) Export delay: base case survives without exports; a model that needs Gulf orders in Year 5 to clear Series B is fragile.

NRE contingency of 25 to 30 percent is normal. Software qualification deserves its own contingency.

**Go-to-market (short).** Domestic reference first — the 87-aircraft competition is winner-take-most with at most two awards. Outside that tranche: named follow-on, emergency lanes (already used for Heron Mk II and Drishti-10), or a service-specific development order. “Pipeline” without paper is not traction. Exports second: air-force ISR, land-border surveillance, or maritime MDA; localisation where Gulf buyers already extract it from Turkish suppliers. Armed-strike marketing travels worse than C5ISR, relay and controller narratives.

**Export lessons (one line each).** Saudi Arabia — multi-billion Akıncı with local production; localisation is the price of entry. UAE — dual-sources; not waiting for an Indian brochure. Philippines — air force led Hermes 900/450 dual-domain ISR (~USD 175 m / 13 airframes). Australia — AIR 7003 MQ-9B cancelled for cyber (REDSPICE); pipelines die from domestic politics as often as from competition.

## 8. Risks and falsifiers


| Risk                                              | Weight      | Mitigant                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Qualification slip                                | Dominant    | Staged certification; named CEMILAC/DGAQA engagement; owned flight-test capacity         |
| Tender loss / order concentration                 | High        | Named path to follow-on, emergency lanes, multi-service capture, exports after reference |
| Commonality erosion across Army/IAF/Navy packages | High        | Configuration disclosure; incremental-qual budgets; refuse silent second airframes       |
| Dual-award price compression                      | Medium-high | Cost structure viable at ₹150 Cr; margin recovered in mission-stack annuity             |
| High-altitude / icing shortfalls on Army package  | Medium      | Demonstrated envelope evidence; propulsion and anti-icing diligence                      |
| Survivability squeeze                             | Medium      | Mission mix weighted to permissive/standoff; open architecture for attritables           |
| Import-dependent BoM and FX                       | Medium      | Path to 60% IC and beyond; escalation clauses                                            |
| Export licensing for C5ISR payloads               | Medium      | Compliance function before first export campaign                                         |
| Key people (certification and flight-test crews)  | Medium      | Retention terms                                                                          |

**Falsifiers**

1. **Attritable endurance breakthrough.** Sub-USD-1M airframe demonstrating 20-plus hours, 200-plus kg multi-sensor payload and SATCOM at production scale.
2. **Satellite substitution.** Proliferated LEO radar/RF constellations with sub-15-minute revisit and real-time tasking at a cost per maintained track that closes land and maritime gaps.
3. **Standoff squeeze.** Long-range SAMs until safe orbits lose sensor utility over frontiers and open water.
4. **Procurement tell.** The 87-aircraft award must convert to contract through 2027; follow-on money must keep funding this class rather than only small drones, HAPS and satellites.
5. **Commonality break.** Separately qualified baselines that duplicate most NRE across propulsion, structure and mission computer/C2 — silent second airframes kill the one-platform economics even if demand remains.

**Known evidence gaps.** No reliable public split of the 87 aircraft by service. Programme value breakdown (aircraft vs ten-year support) varies by outlet. Drishti-10 unit prices are a reported band, not an audited figure.
