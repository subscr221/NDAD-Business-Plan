# Tri-service SCALE UAS business-case rewrite design

## Purpose

Refactor the existing coastal-led SCALE UAS investor case into a researched Indian tri-service case with a focused export comparison. The rewrite will treat the Navy, Army, and Air Force as distinct demand sources for one common aircraft platform with modular mission packages.

The finished document must remain useful to defence-literate lead investors and generalist co-investors. It will be company-agnostic, distinguish sourced facts from reported estimates and internal assumptions, and leave the current drafts untouched.

## Decisions

- Anchor market: India.
- Export comparison: selected Indo-Pacific and Gulf states.
- Product framing: one common SCALE platform, not three separately developed aircraft.
- Financial treatment: rebuild demand, pricing, order cadence, risks, and the illustrative ten-year scenario around tri-service sales.
- Deliverable: a new Markdown rewrite file under `_bmad-output`.
- Style: direct investor prose followed by a humanizer pass; no unsupported promotional language.

## Governing argument

SCALE should be presented as a persistent airborne C5ISR backbone rather than a maritime patrol drone or a general-purpose armed MALE aircraft. A common airframe, mission computer, autonomy stack, datalinks, ground-control architecture, and open payload interfaces can support different service requirements. Service-specific value comes from payloads, software, data integration, doctrine, and sustainment.

The investment thesis remains that qualification and sovereign adoption are the principal gates, while ownership of the mission stack determines long-run margin. The tri-service rewrite must test that thesis rather than assume it. If Army and Air Force evidence does not support the same platform economics, the document will state the limitation.

## Mission architecture

### Indian Army

Research and assess:

- persistent surveillance of borders, mountains, deserts, and depth areas;
- artillery target acquisition, battle-damage assessment, and sensor-to-shooter support;
- communications relay across terrain and beyond line of sight;
- SIGINT, ELINT, and electronic-support missions from standoff or permissive airspace;
- control of launched effects and attritable unmanned systems;
- convoy, formation, and base overwatch where a MALE-class system is operationally justified.

The analysis must distinguish missions suited to a large persistent aircraft from tasks better served by tactical drones, satellites, aerostats, or crewed aviation.

### Indian Air Force

Research and assess:

- persistent standoff ISR and ELINT;
- communications and tactical-data relay;
- strike-package coordination and battle-management support;
- unmanned teaming and control of launched effects;
- surveillance support for air-base and theatre defence;
- peacetime and crisis coverage where persistence matters more than survivability.

The case must not claim that a slow MALE aircraft replaces AEW&C, fighter reconnaissance, or penetrating survivable platforms. It may supplement those assets in permissive or standoff conditions when evidence supports the claim.

### Indian Navy

Retain and tighten the strongest existing material:

- maritime-domain awareness and surface search;
- wide-area patrol and persistent track maintenance;
- communications relay;
- ASW cueing where supported by payload and doctrine evidence;
- control of launched effects;
- support to fleet and coastal commands.

### Shared platform

Describe the common technical and commercial core:

- airframe, propulsion, flight controls, power generation, and endurance;
- military satellite communications and line-of-sight datalinks;
- mission computer, autonomy, data fusion, and ground-control software;
- modular payload interfaces and open mission systems;
- certification evidence and production configuration control;
- training, spares, software support, upgrades, and mission-data services.

Service mission packages will vary in radar, electro-optical sensors, SIGINT/EW payloads, communications equipment, weapons interfaces, and software. The rewrite will identify when those differences create additional qualification cost or weaken commonality.

## Research standard

### Source hierarchy

1. Indian Ministry of Defence, PIB, parliamentary material, service publications, budget and acquisition documents, CEMILAC/DGAQA material, and official tender or procurement notices.
2. Foreign defence ministries, procurement agencies, armed services, government audit offices, and official OEM contract announcements for export comparisons.
3. Established research institutions and specialist publications with attributable reporting.
4. Trade press and market analysts for gaps, ranges, or leads only.

Claims carried only by low-transparency defence aggregators will be corroborated, downgraded to reported claims, or removed.

### Evidence rules

- Attach a source to every time-sensitive procurement, price, quantity, schedule, performance, loss, and market claim.
- Record publication date and access or verification date.
- Separate signed contracts, approved requirements, requests for information, tenders, reported plans, and speculative projections.
- Do not derive a service allocation for the 87-aircraft programme unless a reliable source states it.
- Treat catalogue prices and contract totals carefully; identify whether figures include aircraft, ground stations, weapons, training, infrastructure, logistics, taxes, or sustainment.
- Use ranges when public sources do not support a point estimate.
- Mark the ten-year entrant model as illustrative and show each calculation from stated assumptions.
- Do not add operational claims that reveal or infer sensitive non-public information.

## Export-market comparison

Select a small set of Indo-Pacific and Gulf markets based on documented requirements, procurement activity, mission fit, and plausible access for an Indian supplier. The comparison should cover land-border surveillance, maritime coverage, air-force ISR/relay needs, local-content policy, incumbent platforms, and export-control constraints.

The document will not use a generic total-addressable-market map. Each included country must contribute evidence to a specific mission, transaction, pricing, localisation, or go-to-market point.

## Document structure

1. Executive summary and governing thesis.
2. Why the requirement is tri-service.
3. Class definition: persistent airborne C5ISR backbone.
4. Indian Army mission case.
5. Indian Air Force mission case.
6. Indian Navy mission case.
7. Common-platform architecture and mission packages.
8. Indian procurement evidence, transaction anchors, and demand range.
9. Indo-Pacific and Gulf export comparison.
10. Competitive field and substitution by tactical drones, satellites, aerostats, crewed aircraft, and attritable systems.
11. Qualification, airworthiness, and integration gates.
12. Platform-owner economics, software, sustainment, and upgrades.
13. Rebuilt illustrative ten-year financial scenario and sensitivities.
14. Go-to-market sequence.
15. Risks, falsifiers, and diligence checklist.
16. Glossary and annotated source register.

The final ordering may combine adjacent sections when that improves the argument, but each subject above must be addressed.

## Financial-model design

The model will be rebuilt from disclosed assumptions rather than by increasing the existing unit count.

- Create service demand wedges for Navy, Army, and Air Force.
- Separate domestic anchor orders, follow-on Indian orders, and exports.
- Use transaction-based price ranges, adjusted for configuration scope.
- Model mission-package mix and any recurring software or support revenue separately from aircraft revenue.
- Tie bookings and deliveries to qualification and procurement milestones.
- Include service-specific integration and qualification costs where material.
- Stress qualification slip, order concentration, price compression, payload import dependence, commonality erosion, and export delay.
- Keep signed backlog distinct from pipeline, framework agreements, letters of intent, and addressable demand.
- Do not present the illustrative scenario as a company forecast.

## Editorial requirements

- Preserve accurate claims and useful explanations from the current case.
- Rewrite the complete argument; do not bolt Army and Air Force appendices onto a maritime structure.
- Prefer plain constructions and specific actors.
- Remove significance inflation, generic conclusions, repetitive framing, promotional language, excessive headings, and stock AI transitions.
- Avoid invented anecdotes, quotations, examples, or numerical precision.
- Keep technical acronyms defined on first use and in the glossary.
- Run a final fidelity audit so the humanizer pass does not alter facts, figures, caveats, or source meaning.

## Deliverable and acceptance criteria

The new file will:

- make all three Indian services visible in the executive summary, thesis, demand evidence, mission analysis, financial case, risks, and diligence checklist;
- explain why one common platform can serve them and where commonality stops;
- include a researched Indo-Pacific and Gulf export comparison;
- rebuild rather than merely extend the financial scenario;
- use a traceable source register with claim-level links or notes;
- distinguish facts, estimates, assumptions, and scenarios;
- retain no unsupported claim solely because it appeared in an earlier draft;
- read naturally after a final anti-AI pass;
- leave existing drafts unchanged.
